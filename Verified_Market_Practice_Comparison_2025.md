# IFRS 17 market practice comparison: Hong Kong, Singapore and Canada

**Evidence review date: 30 October 2026. Reporting basis: principally FY2025, with explicitly labelled FY2023 Singapore survey evidence.**

## Executive assessment

Public disclosures support a comparison of named insurers and methodologies, rather than a single prescribed IFRS 17 ILP for each market. The most useful distinctions are the construction of the discount curve, the removal of credit risk, the treatment of liability liquidity, the calibration horizon, and the scope of the reporting entity.

- **Hong Kong:** AIA Group provides unusually useful currency-level numerical disclosures: its 2025 HKD 10-year risk-free rate is 3.10% and the rate including ILP is 3.91%, implying **81 bps ILP**. This is a Group HKD curve, not a verified rate for every Hong Kong product. Its principal method is top-down. [A1, printed p.173 / PDF p.179]
- **Singapore:** Great Eastern Holdings provides a practical bottom-up example, including product scoring, liquidity buckets/application ratios and credit-risk-adjusted reference-asset spreads. Its 2025 policy says market-observable ILP is calculated **at every month-end**. AIA Group's SGD 10-year disclosure implies **64 bps ILP**. These are separate insurer examples, not interchangeable market benchmarks. [A2, printed p.138 / PDF p.140; A1, printed p.173 / PDF p.179]
- **Canada:** The CIA supplies a structured reference methodology, while actual insurer assumptions remain entity-specific. Sun Life discloses removal of both expected and unexpected credit losses; Manulife and Sun Life disclose different RA confidence-level ranges. [A5, pp.26, 35–40; A4, printed pp.69–70 / PDF pp.194–195; A3, printed p.218 / PDF p.220]

**Decision-useful conclusion:** benchmark the internal reinvestment spread and the resulting liability ILP separately. A historical credit-spread percentile, an assumed future asset spread and a final liability discount-rate adjustment are different quantities. Public evidence reviewed here does not establish a common three-market historical averaging window or a universal P10 floor.

The 10-year HKD adjustment decreases by 3 bps year on year, while SGD increases by 12 bps. This illustrates why a single cross-currency “market ILP” is inappropriate. USD curves are relevant to USD liabilities across markets and should not automatically be assigned to the US or Canada.

AIA's reference portfolio is selected for similar characteristics; the adjustment removes risks not relevant to insurance contracts. Curves use observable information to the last reliable market point and extrapolate towards an ultimate forward rate, for example using Smith-Wilson. [A1, same page]

### Great Eastern: a useful calibration-process comparator

The 2025 disclosure describes the following sequence: relevant-currency government risk-free curve; reference-asset market spread adjusted for credit risk; product liquidity assessment using an objective scoring system; allocation to a liquidity bucket/application ratio; application of the corresponding ILP. Observable ILP is calculated at each month-end. [A2, Note 2.26(a), printed p.138 / PDF p.140]

| SGD tenor | GEH 2025 published total discount-rate range |
|---|---:|
| 1 year | 1.41–1.87% |
| 5 years | 1.89–2.36% |
| 10 years | 2.16–2.44% |
| 15 years | 2.23–2.79% |
| 20 years | 2.20–3.04% |

These are **total discount rates**, not quoted ILP ranges. Their widths should not be labelled ILP without establishing that the endpoints use the same RF curve and the relevant zero/full-ILP application assumptions. The reference to RBC2/ICS relates to the disclosed RF/UFR design; it does not mean the entire IFRS 17 ILP calculation is prescribed by the capital framework.

The CIA educational note dated 26 August 2025 specifies parameters effective **15 October 2025**. They are a dated professional reference, not proof that all Canadian insurers apply identical rates or that the parameters remain unchanged in October 2026. [A5 pp.5–6, 35–40]

**The 1–3/4/5+ year categories above are curve tenors, not historical lookback windows.** Intermediate liability liquidity requires judgement. The note also discusses comparison with reference results and aggregate Canadian-dollar unobservable-period present values in the Appointed Actuary reporting context; it should not be described as an unconstrained optional benchmark. [A5 pp.35, 37, 39–40]

The credit-risk discussion distinguishes expected and unexpected losses and considers the limitations of stable through-the-cycle assumptions when current conditions change. It does not establish that a fixed historical credit-spread average is sufficient for every reporting-date liability curve. [A5 p.26]

### Additional verified company examples from online originals

**Prudential plc, FY2025, Note A3:** bottom-up; government RF except HKD swaps. ILP uses corporate yield less RF and credit allowance; 2025 credit allowances are 6–32 bps. Application ratios are 0%, 50% or 100%, reflecting policyholder liquidity characteristics. RA targets the 75th percentile over one year. These are **Group disclosures**, not independently verified PHKL entity disclosures. [W3, “Discount rates” and “Risk adjustment”]

**Canada Life, FY2025:** generally top-down for relevant non-participating business; bottom-up is used for certain liabilities, including those not backed by assets. Credit adjustments consider rating, sector, term, historical information and prevailing conditions. The bottom-up liquidity spread uses an external benchmark. Its RA discussion refers to Lifeco's 85–90% target, net of reinsurance; this is not a Canadian industry target. [W2, printed pp.13, 23–24 / PDF pp.15, 25–26]

## 4. Risk adjustment: comparable fields and limits

| Insurer | Method | Disclosed confidence level | Scope / important qualification | Exact source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AIA Group | Confidence-level technique | 75th percentile | Geographic segment basis; no one-year horizon established by the cited passage | A1 p.174/PDF180, Note 24 |
| GEH Group | Confidence-level technique | 85th percentile | Underlying-contract RA does not consider the effect of reinsurance | A2 p.139/PDF141, Note 2.26 |
| Manulife | Margin approach | 90–95% | All segments; margins reviewed periodically; discount basis consistent with best estimates | A3 p.218/PDF220 |
| Sun Life | Margins on best-estimate assumptions | Approximately 85–90% | Margins generally 5–20%, varying by uncertainty and business | A4 p.70/PDF195 |

For Sun Life, **5–20% is an assumption-margin range**. It is not a confidence level and not an RA/BEL ratio. A higher disclosed confidence level does not by itself establish greater prudence: calibration horizon, diversification, risk aggregation, distribution and reinsurance treatment must also be comparable.

## 7. Implications for the internal reinvestment-spread study

The earlier internal materials describe historical spreads, reinvestment assumptions and final ILP impacts. They are useful internal evidence but are not independent market-practice evidence. See the separate [Internal Slides Study Record](/Users/elvirazhong/Documents/ChatGPT/Elvira/output/ilp-research/Internal_Slides_Study_Record.md).

1. **Build a spread bridge:** reference yield → comparable RF → gross spread → credit-risk deduction → liquidity compensation → liability application ratio → final ILP. Reconcile the internal reinvestment assumption to the final liability adjustment.
2. **Align the benchmark:** HKD swap spreads cannot be compared mechanically with USD Treasury or Canadian government spreads. Match currency, tenor/duration, rating, asset mix, date and net/gross credit treatment.
3. **Separate historical evidence from current calibration:** a three-, five- or ten-year study may support a long-run reinvestment assumption. It does not alone substantiate the reporting-date liability ILP. Show the current-market reconciliation and any transition to long-run assumptions.
4. **Treat internal floors as internal choices:** the reviewed sources do not establish a universal ten-year P10 floor. Document why such a floor is appropriate, what quantity it constrains and whether it changes the final liability curve.
5. **Use company evidence selectively:** GEH is useful for liquidity scoring and update frequency; Sun Life for credit-risk deductions and lapse study period; AIA for directly derivable currency ILPs; CIA for a transparent reference methodology and extrapolation parameters.

Suggested management wording:

> Public evidence supports multiple IFRS 17 discount-rate approaches and entity-specific liquidity calibration. Our comparison therefore distinguishes gross reinvestment spreads from liability ILP and benchmarks credit-risk deductions, liability liquidity, update frequency and extrapolation separately. Historical averaging and percentile floors require their own justification; the reviewed evidence does not establish a uniform market convention across Hong Kong, Singapore and Canada.
